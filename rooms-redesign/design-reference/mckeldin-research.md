# McKeldin Library reference notes

- [University of Maryland Libraries, building overview](https://www.lib.umd.edu/about/overview): confirms McKeldin as the main library.
- [Maryland Today, architectural molding survey](https://today.umd.edu/new-exhibit-frames-architectures-unsung-hero): describes the Georgian detail of the original east entrance and the plainer later expansion around the corner.
- [UMD historic preservation survey, page 80 onward](https://api.drum.lib.umd.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/448d91a1-c9a5-45d9-9b4f-f29ef7cccf0e/content): documents the original library and later addition, with exterior views of their different brick façades.
- [Maryland Today, building proportions](https://today.umd.edu/take-this-class-summer-edition-the-secret-life-of-buildings): the seven interior floors read as four exterior stories because of the oversized windows.
- [Facilities Management building photograph and record](https://www.facilities.umd.edu/node/290): front elevation shows the pale belt course, shallow dark roof over the original east block, six columns, three arched entries, and a round oculus in the pediment.
- The local `campus-aerial-core.jpg` (Maryland MD iMAP source in `campus-aerial.SOURCE.txt`) shows the building's stepped rectangular footprint, shallow sloped perimeter around flat roof decks, and rooftop service structures. It is a design reference and is never rendered.

Model decisions: retain the mapped footprint and east-facing portico, distinguish the west addition with a simpler brick skin, add four tall tiers of Georgian-trimmed windows to the original block, and give its shallow roof and pediment an identifiable profile. The [architectural survey's McKeldin analysis](https://api.drum.lib.umd.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/448d91a1-c9a5-45d9-9b4f-f29ef7cccf0e/content#page=60) describes the west addition's matching hipped gray slate roof, full-height narrow black aluminum and glass bands on three sides, and two pale limestone courses. The sloped slate is modeled as a low perimeter around the flat service decks visible in the aerial, with modest mechanical units. The mapped east projection includes the open porch: the tall brick mass ends behind it, while the footprint continues as a low stone podium beneath the columns. Dimensions are approximate where published measured elevations were unavailable.
