# North Hill historic residence halls

UMD [Residential Facilities' Anne Arundel page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/anne-arundel-hall) documents a three-floor 1937 hall and a student lounge in its cupola. The official exterior photo shows brick wings, a white central entrance with a triangular pediment, slate roof dormers, a square white tower, open lantern and dark dome. The native Three.js model uses the mapped long north-south footprint and adds those features to its east-facing main elevation. The tagged 11 m height is retained for the main walls; the cupola rises above it. The entrance orientation and facade spacing are visual interpretations of the photo and campus layout.

UMD [Residential Facilities' Queen Anne's page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/queen-annes-hall) documents four floors. Its exterior photo shows a broad brick front gable, four white entrance columns, raised steps, slate roofs and projecting white dormers. The native Three.js model follows the mapped bent footprint with roofs over its two wings, a gable at the north-side entrance, and its own four-floor window rhythm. The imported 14.8 m height is treated as the main roof silhouette, not a surveyed facade height.

UMD [Residential Facilities' Dorchester page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/dorchester-hall) documents a four-floor hall. Its exterior photo shows a broad brick end gable, pale raking cornice, upper windows, a small attic vent, a limestone-framed central entrance, a raised landing with stairs on both sides, slate roofs and dormers. [JMT Architecture's project description](https://jmtarchitecture.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/General-Higher-Ed-Brochure_Feb2024.pdf) calls it a four-story Georgian building and documents the brick, limestone and historically correct replacement windows. The native model follows the mapped L footprint and puts the entrance gable on the west end facing the small plaza. The doorway orientation is inferred from the site geometry and aerial imagery; the photograph itself is not georeferenced.

UMD [Residential Facilities' Somerset page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/somerset-hall) shows the hall's full-height four-column white portico, pediment, broad entrance stairs, red brick wings, slate roof and small dormers. Its building-info panel says five floors while its history calls the building three stories; the model follows the three prominent facade window rows visible in the exterior photo, plus roof dormers. Separate roof volumes preserve the mapped U-shaped footprint and south-facing open court. The north-facing portico position is inferred from the footprint, paths and aerial imagery.

UMD [Residential Facilities' Caroline page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/caroline-hall) documents four floors. Its exterior photo shows a long red-brick facade with three clear wall-window tiers, an extended pale cornice, a slate hipped roof with repeated white dormers, and a restrained pale entrance. The model uses its long east-west mapped footprint, custom windows and dormers on both roof slopes. The north-facing courtyard entrance placement follows the visible paths in the aerial image and is an approximation; the official image itself has no bearing metadata.

UMD [Residential Facilities' Worcester page](https://drf.umd.edu/facilities/residence-halls-communities/worcester-hall) documents a four-story hall. Its exterior image emphasizes a raised brick entry landing, two stairs running outward along the facade, white entrance pilasters and ornament, an octagonal window above, and a lower ground-level door. The native model preserves those features on the long west facade and follows the mapped north-south hall and eastward south wing with two slate roofs. The [OSM building way](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/23546215) includes an entrance node tagged `building_entrance=steps` at 38.9846244, -76.9451169 on its west edge; the model's front landing is aligned to it. The source map snapshot misspells the building name as “Worchester Hall”; the official name and residence search use Worcester.
